Why Do We Trick-or-Treat?

The tradition of going door-to-door for treats evolved across four distinct historical eras:

1. Food Offerings for Spirits (500 BC): Ancient Celts left bowls of cider, nuts, and harvest food on their doorsteps to appease roaming Samhain ghosts so they wouldn't enter or curse the household.
2. Souling (Middle Ages): On All Souls' Day, poor citizens and children went door-to-door asking for food. Homeowners gave them spiced "soul cakes" in exchange for singing prayers for the souls of dead relatives.
3. Guising (19th Century Scotland & Ireland): Young people dressed up in disguises ("guises") and visited neighbors' homes. Instead of just receiving hand-outs, children had to earn treats by performing a trick, reciting poetry, singing songs, or telling jokes.
4. Bribing Pranksters (1920s–1950s America): Early American Halloween was infamous for destructive pranks (like unhinging fence gates or soaping windows). Neighborhood committees began organizing community "trick-or-treat" candy events as a friendly way to bribe kids into choosing sweet treats over mischievous tricks!
